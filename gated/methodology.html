Methodology

Exactly what the platform measures, how the scores are produced, and what the current backtest says about how often the predictions are right.

1. Universe and data sources

Coverage: ~5,500 NSE/BSE names. The author does not curate — every ticker SWS tracks shows up unless an NSE surveillance flag (ASM/GSM) blocks it.

  • Simply Wall Street (SWS) — Snowflake score, fair-value upside, V3 100-pt breakdown, 90-day news briefs.
  • Yahoo Finance — live quotes, earnings history (~494 covered names for fundamentals trajectory).
  • NSE (scraped locally) — event calendar, corporate announcements, bulk/block deals, surveillance flags.
  • Groq / Gemini LLMs — qualitative news classifier for the earnings predictor. Heuristic keyword fallback when keys are absent (UI surfaces a banner when this happens).

2. SWS Picks — how a stock becomes a recommendation

The picks come straight from SWS's V3 framework (100-pt composite of Future, Past, Health, Dividends, Valuation). The platform doesn't re-score — it bands the SWS score into STRONG BUY → BUY → HOLD → TRIM → SELL bands and pairs each with the live fair-value upside %. The bands are conservative: STRONG BUY requires score ≥ 75 + upside ≥ 30% together, not either-or.

Hard gates that REMOVE a stock from picks regardless of score:

  • NSE Surveillance (ASM/GSM) flag — surfaces a loud red banner instead of a recommendation.
  • Governance issues (pledged-share spike, auditor exit) flagged by the governance gate.
  • Fundamentals gate failures (NaN/zero/unparseable critical metrics).

3. Earnings Predictor — 9-component score (max ±100)

For every upcoming NSE earnings event in the next 30 days, the predictor produces a BEAT / INLINE / MISS verdict with a confidence bucket. The score is a weighted sum of nine components; each can contribute positive or negative points. The verdict is just a threshold on the composite (≥65 → BEAT, <35 → MISS, else INLINE).

4. Confidence cap + the calibration gate

Confidence is hard-capped at 65% in V1. The cap will not lift until the backtest clears three conditions simultaneously:

  • ≥ 30 resolved actuals (currently 47 ✓)
  • 60–64% bucket hit-rate ≥ 55%
  • Brier score < 0.20

Until the gate clears, every BEAT-or-MISS card carries an explicit "capped at 65%" note. The backtest re-runs nightly and writes data/catalysts/earnings-backtest-latest.json. You can also click any prediction in the Track Record tab to see the calibration plot.

5. Backtest discipline

Predictions are archived per day in data/catalysts/earnings-history/<YYYY-MM-DD>.json. Each row is keyed on (symbol, event_iso_date) and dedup is fiscal-quarter-end based to dodge IST/UTC off-by-one bugs. Restatements (NSE revising the actual) preserve the prior value in actual_history[]. Predictor version is stamped into every row so a weight change can't blur into the prior version's hit rate.

Cross-validation: walk-forward by fiscal quarter (rolling-origin). A random 80/20 hold-out leaks future information into training for time-series data; walk-forward trains on quarters 1..k and tests on quarter k+1, advancing the window. Bootstrap 95% confidence intervals are reported on every hit-rate metric — the platform refuses to claim "55%" when n is too small to distinguish that from random.

6. Portfolio analyser — what the action grid actually does

The analyser hard-gates on the 3-question risk profile (CONSERVATIVE / MODERATE / AGGRESSIVE). Without it, the analyser refuses to score and shows the survey instead. With it, every position gets:

  • Action — REDUCE / TRIM / HOLD / TOP-UP / BUY based on combined score, macro regime, and concentration.
  • Sell trigger — concrete rupee stop = max(avg cost × 0.85, current × 0.92, 52w low × 1.01). The most conservative of the three floors wins.
  • Recovery math — break-even price + upside-needed % + recovery probability heuristic.
  • Position sizing — earned weight vs current weight (over/right-sized/under).

7. What the platform does NOT do

  • Issue personalised investment advice (not registered as a SEBI RA/IA).
  • Handle or hold client money or securities.
  • Guarantee any return, hit-rate, or risk-adjusted outcome.
  • Pay any third-party for inclusion or favourable scoring — see the holdings disclosure for the full conflict-of-interest statement.
